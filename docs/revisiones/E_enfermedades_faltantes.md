Caveats that must be resolved before coding (all are properties of the source, not transcription errors):
1. **TMP-SMX component.** The tables never say whether "8-10 mg/kg/día" refers to trimethoprim. Do not implement without a clinical decision (the two WHO documents and NICE all express this drug per trimethoprim component).
2. **Gentamicin "Dosis máxima: 2000 mg/día"** (Table 3, lower UTI) is implausible for an aminoglycoside and should not be coded; the document offers no other gentamicin ceiling.
3. **Cefazolin/cefuroxime dose cell.** In Table 3 "ITU Alta" one merged cell ("100-150 mg/kg/día IV cada 8 horas / Dosis usual: >30 kg 750 mg IV cada 8 horas / Dosis máxima: 1,5 gramos IV cada 8 horas") sits beside both "Cefazolina" and "Cefuroxima (Zinacef)"; it is not possible to tell from the page which drug the 750 mg / 1,5 g figures belong to.
4. The document states that "En Colombia no existe ningún consenso" before it and that its recommendations were adapted from four guidelines (AAP, Spanish Ministry of Health, McTaggart, NICE) by expert consensus; choice must follow "la sensibilidad antimicrobiana local".

**(b) AIEPI-2012** (2 months–5 years) has no outpatient UTI regimen; any child whose urinalysis suggests infection is treated as pyelonephritis and referred:
- p. 535 (PDF 537): "Si hay posibilidad de realizar parcial de orina y es compatible con infección urinaria, se debe remitir, por la alta probabilidad de ser una PIELONEFRITIS; iniciar manejo antibiótico apropiado, referirlo con primera dosis de Ceftriaxona, igual dosis que la recomendada para el niño con ENFERMEDAD FEBRIL DE ALTO RIESGO."

### 2.3 Disagreements (UTI)
- **Who is referred.** AIEPI 2012 refers every under-5 with a urinalysis compatible with UTI after one dose of ceftriaxone; COL-ITU-2023 prefers oral treatment and reserves IV/hospital care for listed criteria (< 3 months, fever ≥ 38,5 °C at 3-6 months, toxic appearance, vomiting, etc.).
- **Ceftriaxone dose.** AIEPI 100 mg/kg once daily; COL-ITU-2023 75-100 mg/kg/día, maximum 2 g/día; WHO 80 mg/kg/dose once daily; NICE 50–80 mg/kg once daily (maximum 4 g per day).
- **Cefalexin dose.** COL-ITU-2023 75-100 mg/kg/día in 4 doses (max 2000 mg/día for pyelonephritis) versus NICE 12.5 mg/kg twice a day (25 mg/kg two to four times a day only "for severe infections").
- **Nitrofurantoin dose.** COL-ITU-2023 5-7 mg/kg/día every 6 h; NICE 750 micrograms/kg four times a day; WHO 1 mg/kg/dose every 6 h or 2 mg/kg/dose every 12 h.
- **Amoxicillin-clavulanate dose.** COL-ITU-2023 40-45 mg/kg/día; WHO 80–90 mg/kg/day of amoxicillin component.
- **First-line agent for cystitis.** COL-ITU-2023 lists TMP-SMX, nitrofurantoin or cefalexin as first line and amoxicillin-clavulanate as second line; WHO lists amoxicillin-clavulanate among equal options and warns that "Resistance to sulfamethoxazole+trimethoprim is high in many settings"; NICE gives trimethoprim only "if there is a low risk of resistance".
- **Oral agent for pyelonephritis.** COL-ITU-2023 and NICE: cefalexin (first-generation cephalosporin); WHO: ciprofloxacin for mild-moderate cases.
- **Duration.** Cystitis: 3 to 5 days (Colombia) vs 3 days (NICE) vs 3–5 days depending on drug (WHO). Pyelonephritis: 7 to 10 days (Colombia, NICE) vs 7 days (WHO).

### 6.2 International references
- **WHO-AWaRe-2022**, Table 26.4 (p. 358), meningitis, children: "Cefotaxime (IV): 50 mg/kg/dose given every 8 hours OR Ceftriaxone (IV): 100 mg/kg given once a day"; duration "Unknown pathogen 10 days (adults and children) … Confirmed pneumococcal meningitis 10–14 days; Confirmed meningococcal meningitis 5–7 days". ### 6.3 Disagreements
- Empirical sepsis: AIEPI ceftriaxone 100 mg/kg/día; WHO 2022 first choice ampicillin + gentamicin, ceftriaxone 80 mg/kg second choice.
- Mastoiditis: AIEPI ceftriaxone 100 mg/kg/día; WHO Pocket Book cloxacillin/flucloxacillin 50 mg/kg every 6 h or ceftriaxone, 10 days total.
- Ampicillin at 2-3 months: AIEPI 200 mg/kg/día in four doses; MINSALUD-MENINGO-2018 400 mg/kg divided every 6 h; WHO 50 mg/kg/dose every 8 h.
- Cefotaxime: MINSALUD-MENINGO-2018 300 mg/kg divided every 6 h; WHO 50 mg/kg/dose every 8 h (2022) or every 6 h (2013).
